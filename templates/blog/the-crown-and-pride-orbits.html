People keep asking how a Sekarri pride actually works day to day. Who is close to who? Who is in charge? Here is the full picture.

The Shape of a Pride

Sekarri births run seven females to every male, so a typical pride is one male and seven females. The seven are not all equally close to him. They usually break down like this:

  • Velara (usually 2) — the closest to the male romantically. This is the real love triangle of a pride.
  • Lorena (usually 3) — the middle ground. They have varying degrees of relations with the male, but are more enriched by love of the group, and sometimes a more intimate love with each other.
  • Astraria (usually 2) — the furthest out. Often away by duty or by choice, and sometimes floating between prides.

The Cordara

Within the Velara, one woman (two at most, but usually one) is the male's single closest love. The Sekarri word for her is the Cordara — “Heart-bound”.

Dating a Pride

Pride Love

Above all, the eight members relate most to the group love they share with each other. Affection runs between everyone — female to female, male to female. When a pride cooks together they will share kisses and hugs as they go. On special occasions the male may be shared with the outer females, if they all wish it, but mostly it is a constant kind of pride love where everyone is affectionate with each other anyway.

Festive occasions are common. The classic one: the Astraria comes home, and all eight cook dinner together.

The Crown

The Crown is the centre of the pride — the one responsible for its stability. She is usually a female. It can be the male; that is just rarer. In the olden days the Crown was thought of as a kind of modern Alpha-Female.

  • Legal head. She signs the formal documents, house deeds and so on.
  • Traditionally not one of the women closest to the male. Closeness to him trades off against control of the pride. In modern times the roles blur, and some Crowns are Velara too — see below.
  • Host. She organises the gatherings and does her best to make sure the Astraria attend.
  • Negotiator. When a pride starts to fracture, the Crown steps in. That can mean cutting off an Astraria who is being divisive, or making the Velara and the male see reason.

Three Orbits — and the True Centre

From the male's point of view a pride is three orbits: Velara on the inside, then Lorena, then Astraria — each progressively more emotionally distant from him.

But that is only his view. In reality, the true orbits are around the Crown. A pride has two centres of gravity: the Cordara anchors the romance, and the Crown anchors the pride itself. The rule of thumb: the closer you are to the man, the further you are from control of the pride.

How the Crown Changed

  • Age of Alpha females. No Crown existed. The Alpha sat beside the male, but only superficially — her attention was on hunting and holding her position.
  • Traditional era (thousands of years ago). The Crown was the furthest stable member from the male — a Lorena, never an Astraria. She was the literal centre: the Astraria orbited her on one side, the male and his Velara sat on the other. When parties fell out, she decided, and both sides wanted her good graces. Roles were set in stone.
  • Modern era. Life is easier, so people move freely between roles — and it gets messy. A “rockstar” can take Crown, Cordara and the male all at once. That usually ends in divorce or constant restructuring. Average families still run close to the traditional shape; the Crown is more administrative now, but keeps its prestige.

Not every woman suits every orbit. Kira and Camellia are carefree and career-first; men are more of a treat and a stress relief, which makes them natural Astraria. Fel and Tabby are both built for intimate connection with a male, yet they would fit a pride very differently. Fel would be Crown and Cordara both — she is an alpha female and she demands it. Tabby is cute and read by the pride as no threat, so she could easily be the Cordara: traditionally never the Crown, though in modern times nothing rules it out.

And one thing that is hard to communicate: the women on the outer orbits are not lesbian or bisexual as such, though some individuals lean that way. The main thing is the group love, which every member feels.

The Jewel

Certain cultures and nations have worked around the Crown's burden legally with a role called the Jewel: a high-powered woman who gets more of what she wants from a pride without being legally responsible for it. In practice it is something agents write into contracts for rich, wealthy women.

It rests on a misreading of older, more isolated cultures. In their part of the world Crowns died often — hunting, infighting, cross-tribal confrontations — and the Jewel was a kind of backup Crown, ready to take the centre when the Crown fell. In modern times the Jewel is, to some, the de facto Crown: all of the pull, none of the paperwork.
