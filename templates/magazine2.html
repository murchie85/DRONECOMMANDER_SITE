150 characters and counting

Posted 20 & 15 August · Milestone report

The cast list just crossed a number most studios never reach: over 150 fleshed-out characters, from Freexyl side-crew to Deritonian priestesses.

More than 100 core characters now carry fully rendered art, sprites and backstories — and the supporting cast keeps growing on top. The trick to the pace: NPCs are drawn without the final rendering layer, which gets them done in about 40% of the time of a fully fledged piece like Fel or Kira.

Behind it sits a new Timeline tool bolted onto the in-game dev tracker (the purple button, top right of the title screen). It maps every major event, branch and character beat so the whole game can be planned at a high level — "the lore is as deep as a 40-year running sci-fi epic," and now there's a chart to prove it.

Current board: Nyrissa 80% · Marta's Passion 80% · hostage-rescue cutscene 80% · Amu's Cave 70% · Albion's Anchor 65% · Daria's first meeting 10% · FNS Ember 10%.

What I'm working on

The Vionites

Right now the focus is building out the Vionites — the most powerful known race in the galaxy. They sit dead on the line of the Tier system: top of Tier 1, transitioning into Tier 2 on the back of their recent grasp of Resonant Essence. They're the only Tier 1 race that can cross space without dispersion fields.

Their empire runs to ~100 billion citizens across multiple star systems: militaristic mouse-like generals in the Core Worlds, politicians, retail and luxury in the Home Worlds, and hippie squirrel-like populations out on the Fringe. Their energy footprint is too big to hide, so they prepared for war — and they're the only race insane enough to go toe-to-toe with the Drone Fleet.

Off duty, on purpose

Rexalia · Voidblade Sentinel · 7ft 7 / 2.31m

The 1 August post put it plainly: lots of lore, but the TLDR is you vote on a new (extra) costume for her. 137 likes later, the answer is the Evening Grove dress — red silk, gold rings, a braids-down variant that followed on 23 August.

It matters because of who wears it. Rexalia is a warrior of the Aetherialis, the Honnehari's answer to the Vanguard. When her leaders prepared to leave the galaxy, she was the one ordered to shut down the Veyr — the invariant beacons that read and shaped reality across the sector. She called the departure a betrayal. Then she did it anyway, beacon by beacon, until the galaxy read as though her people had never existed.

She wakes now with fragmented memories and unfinished feelings, and one question above the war, above Straker, above everything: what became of her people? The dress is a night off from that. She's earned one.

Neph: the warlord

Supreme leader of the Dominion · primary antagonist

Towering, crimson-maned, spiked armour, a throne — every inch the warlord. Neph made early contact with Earth and cultivated the shadow government that became the Foundation of Man, offering them a homeworld in exchange for help annexing Earth. Before the evacuation of Alara she abducted the semi-sentient Carapax and converted them into her armies.

Publicly she demands 1 million humans to "pay off" the Dark Worlders. The real ask was 100,000. The gap is skim — an "accounting error" in her favour — and a private confession reveals she means to top it up every five years. It was never a one-time tribute.

Before the throne

Neph & Kat

Long before the Dominion, Neph was a girl with a best friend. Kat — today a combat specialist stuck training rookies — was as close to her as anyone has ever been. Kat's brother Bas was competent, handsome, and enjoyed Neph's company; and every Sekarri girl adores her brother, because his friends are the boys she might one day meet.

Neph wanted the three of them in one pride — her, Kat and Bas. Under pride law that would put Kat in a pride with her own brother, and Kat refused. Vehemently. It wasn't the moment Neph went off the rails — but it was the first crack, the beginning of a warped reading of history that she has never stopped writing.

Character file

Vess Avraxian

7'9", chief investor in the Black Fleet. An ex-pirate who made her fortune by shady means and was far more ruthless than Morrigan in her prime. She fronted the cash for Morrigan's ships — "Without me there'd be no Black Fleet" — and lets Morrigan take the limelight, right up until she doesn't: "I AM THE BOSS of this whole show — got it?"

She holds the rare Shards — dispersion material of processed strange matter — and she knows exactly what you and "that silver AI wench" are doing with one. Everyone is on her payroll. Even Morrigan. Her bodyguard, incidentally, was snatched from one of Neph's safehouses.

Character file

Kaelix

7'8", the Freexyl's granddaughter — and a clone. The original Kaelix was a hero of the Genetic Wars: she burned down the Caelin labs, died of her wounds, and broadcast the evidence to the world first. Then the Langarans bought her body and grew the Kaelix series from her DNA.

The one aboard now is a digital variant of that mind in a cloned body — acquired by Neph, her priorities corrupted, then deprogrammed by Vess's people. "Same way you humans reprogram each other: media, ideology. Neph has simply mastered and automated it." Beneath the layers, the hero who torched the labs is still trying to get out. You can date her. It isn't easy.

Q&A: Ninefold Drift

Jan C. · composer · Germany · ambient & upbeat exploration tracks for the RPG segments

Who are we listening to out there?

"Among your favourite tunes while exploring, you might find a few tracks by me, Jan C., aka Ninefold Drift. It's a pleasure to meet you all! I'm a 25-year-old composer from Germany."

How does a biology student end up scoring a space opera?

"During my studies of Biology and Educational Science, I've found myself focusing quite a bit on aesthetics and, thus... music."

What do games mean to you?

"To me, games and well-designed art in general can feel like self-contained worlds where we can experience possibilities that don't exist in our everyday lives and learn from them."

Where will players hear you?

"For Drone Commander, I had the great opportunity to compose some music for the journey you are about to be thrown into. You'll mainly hear my tunes scattered throughout the RPG's exploration segments: ancient ruins, cursed villages, and icy mountains. So I suppose I'm now partially responsible for your auditory experience. No pressure at all, right?..."

Parting words?

"I hope you enjoy my little contributions to the game. See you out there! :)"

Lead composer

Evgeny Ryabovol

The main musician on Drone Commander — Evgeny writes the bulk of the score. Composer, sound designer and audio producer, Kyiv-based, ten-plus years in game and film audio, 200+ projects, and a taste for orchestral scores crossed with synth-rock. When the fleet moves, the boss drops or the trailer hits, that's him.

Hear his work at ryabovol.com. Full interview next issue.

Straight talk · from the dev

"$10,000 per track, please"

The plan was to hire a small, unheard-of band with vocals for a couple of promo tracks — that Titan A.E. cinematic feel for the trailers. Found one, eleven likes or so. They said yes instantly. Then the agent stepped in: "$10,000 per track, please." For context, even Hollywood would walk unless you're Iron Maiden.

The misconception is that everyone's got money, including big companies. The truth is the bigger the company, the less they pay — ask any boyband member what they earn per month, or a LOTR cast member how far the payout lasted. That was Samwise; imagine the elf on the wall.

The advice: don't be offended when someone says a number way beyond you. And if their number sounds insane, it probably is — nobody's paying $10k per track plus another $10k to licence it; those are top-5% Hollywood numbers. Most people have no idea how to value themselves, or the people they represent. If you're building something, people come onboard for a reasonable sum or they don't. Plenty more fish in the sea. (And when it comes to rock bands, the average Norwegian plumber has a savage metal band.)

How it works

Free the corrupted entropy

The Dark Worlders' Forges are designed to take the souls of living beings and refine them into higher-dimensional consumable resources. The side product is the entropy left over — corrupted. That's where Entropy Spawn come from.

Destroy Entropy Spawn and you can deposit their corrupted entropy at the Spire. In return you get Echo Fragments, which convert into Mirror Shards for the Entropy Lattice upgrade menu. Collect enough to fill the lattice and you unlock a secret ending.

Enemy design · open call

Dark Worlder foot soldiers

One of the last big enemies left to design — and one the dev has been "very, very reluctant" to do. Think evil, ethereal blood angels: red, space-marine-like troopers. The reluctance is the problem itself: who can stand against them?

Concept art in progress shows two Dark Troopers decimating a combined US, Chinese and European assault, snapping the necks of Sekarri and humans alike. Designs welcome — the dev is open to iterating on yours.

Cutting-room floor

Lore that didn't ship

  • Morrigan was Neph. In the original draft Morrigan was the main villain: she'd stolen Dark Worlder technology and was building a totalitarian empire from a secret base on Venus. It never shipped because the plan was always for her to turn good — her role in Straker's future was too important — so she became an "alternate good" instead, and the warlord seat went to Neph.
  • Amu and Straker, spiritual twins. Kane and Ivakan merge their DNA with a ship AI to create Amu, while on Earth a three-parent cure produces Straker — both from one split resonant-essence source. The twin ending was cut; a "below-universe" link between them survives.
